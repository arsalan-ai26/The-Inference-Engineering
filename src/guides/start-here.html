Guide · Chapter 0

From agent builder to inference engineer

If you build agents and LLM applications, you already use inference every day, through an API. This page maps what you know to what the book teaches, gives a one-month fast track and the full order, and ends with a table that sends you to the right chapter when something breaks.

Three habits from application work carry over directly. Measure before you change anything (chapter 6.1 is the inference version of a load test). Treat every number from a vendor or a paper as a claim to check. And keep prompts stable: the single most effective inference optimization an agent builder controls is a prefix that never changes.

2. How the book works

  • Each chapter starts with a skip check. If you can answer its three questions, skim the chapter and do the lab.
  • Labs run on a CPU or a free Colab GPU. Every result shown was run, and labelled as measured; parts that need bigger GPUs are labelled as expected behaviour. Chapter 1.6 and the compute guide cover renting a GPU when you need one.
  • Numbers are computed, not estimated by hand. Worked examples show the code that produced them; change the inputs and rerun.
  • Every chapter ends with references and notes on sources. The reading list collects all the papers; the glossary and formula sheet collect the terms and equations.

4. The full order

Read the parts in order; each builds on the ones before. At about 10 to 15 hours a week the whole book takes about 31 weeks: Part 1 (math, PyTorch and neural nets, 2.5 weeks), Part 2 (transformer inference mechanics, 3.5), Part 3 (engines and serving, 3.5), Part 4 (model optimization, 5), Part 5 (GPUs and kernels, 4), Part 6 (production, 5), Part 7 (post-training, 3) and Part 8 (specialized workloads, engine internals and research, 5), ending with the capstone in chapter 8.5.
