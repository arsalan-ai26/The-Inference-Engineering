Guide

After the book: kernel engineering and research engineering

The book prepares you for applied inference roles. Two specializations need more: kernel engineering (writing the GPU code that engines call) and research engineering (turning research ideas into working, measured systems and models). This guide gives each track a staged path, the best free resources we found, and the portfolio evidence hiring teams look for. Every link was checked on 8 November 2026.

1. Kernel engineering

The job: write and tune the kernels behind attention, GEMMs, MoE routing, quantization and communication, for one or more GPU generations; read profiler output fluently; and know when a compiler (Triton, torch.compile) is good enough and when hand-written CUDA, CUTLASS or a tile DSL is needed. In the book: chapters 5.1 to 5.8, especially 5.4 and 5.8, and chapter 4.8's DeepSeek kernels.

Portfolio evidence for kernel roles
  • A public worklog of one kernel from naive to tuned, each step measured against a vendor library, with Nsight Compute screenshots.

  • Correctness tests against a reference across shapes and dtypes, and honest baselines (the KernelBench-Verified lesson).

2. Research engineering

The job: build the training, evaluation and inference infrastructure researchers use; implement ideas from papers fast and correctly; run careful experiments and ablations; and know when a result is real. It needs breadth across modeling, training and systems, plus experimental discipline. In the book: chapter 8.4 (reproducing a paper), 8.5 (the capstone), Part 7, and the optional math chapters 1.7 to 1.10.

Portfolio evidence for research engineering roles
  • A reproduction write-up (chapter 8.4's template) with code, configs and raw results, including the parts that did not reproduce.
  • A from-scratch model trained to a reported quality, with the training curves and the bugs you found along the way.
  • An ablation study with error bars that changes a conclusion, even a small one.
  • Infrastructure others used: an evaluation harness, a data pipeline, a training or rollout speedup, merged into an open project.

3. For both tracks

  • Read primary sources. The reading list collects every paper the book cites; DeepSeek's kernel repositories and design notes (chapters 4.8 and 5.8) are some of the best published kernel and systems engineering.
  • Work in public. Worklogs, reproduction reports and merged pull requests are the evidence both kinds of team look for.
  • Budget GPU time by stage, as in chapter 8.5: develop on CPU or a small GPU and rent large GPUs only for measurement runs.
